# TJ Construction

## The Brief

TJ Construction had done the hard part. Years of solid work, a strong local reputation in the North West, and a growing list of completed projects. What they hadn't done was build a brand and digital presence to match.

The goal was clear: stop competing for small local jobs and start winning larger construction contracts — extensions, commercial builds, the kind of work that requires a company to look the part before anyone picks up the phone. The existing website and branding were rooted firmly in the local trades world. We were brought in to move them out of it.

## The Problem

The existing website was a relic. Built in the early 2000s and untouched since, it communicated exactly the wrong things to exactly the right audience. The owner was already running Google Ads and driving traffic — the pipeline existed. But people were landing on a page that looked like an old Facebook profile and leaving. The trust that the ads were buying was being burned on contact.

The branding told the same story. The logo was a T and a J arranged into a house shape — a design language shared by every local joinery and trades company in the country. Functional. Forgettable. Not the identity of a company that wins large-scale construction contracts.

The gap wasn't between TJ and their competition. It was between TJ and the companies they aspired to become.

## The Approach

We started with research — looking at the companies the owner pointed to as aspirational benchmarks. The pattern was consistent: bigger construction firms move away from obvious trade iconography and toward identity. Bold typography. Abstract mark-making. Colour used with confidence rather than convention.

The brief became: build TJ an identity that could sit alongside those companies without apology.

The core colours stayed — there was equity in them worth keeping. But the logo was reimagined entirely. Construction is about stacking, building, layering — bricks, blocks, structure emerging from repetition. The new mark leaned into abstraction: geometric forms that referenced the built environment without spelling it out. Blueprint-inspired pattern work. The logo itself used as a structural motif, repeated and stacked like a Jenga tower of identity.

Big, bold, unambiguous. Less local trader, more national contractor.

## The Work

A full brand identity system and a ground-up website rebuild.

The site was built on the MM stack — Next.js, TypeScript, Tailwind, deployed on Vercel. Clean, fast, and built to do a specific job: convert a visitor into an enquiry. Every page feeds toward a contact form. The project showcase gives the work room to breathe — detailed breakdowns of completed builds, supplier information, photography — everything a client needs to build trust before they reach out. A gallery surfaces the best of the portfolio directly.

The brand system delivered alongside it: logo suite, colour palette, typography, pattern library. A coherent identity, packaged and handed over in full.

The site is live and receiving enquiries. The assets are in place for whenever the full rollout happens.

## The Outcome

A modern, confident digital presence where previously there was none. Contact form enquiries arriving consistently through a site that finally reflects the quality of the work behind it.

The brand system exists in full — ready to be rolled out across every touchpoint when the time comes. The groundwork is done. The direction is set.

For MM, this was the first project out of the studio. We treated it with the same intention we bring to everything — and it shows.
