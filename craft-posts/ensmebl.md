# Ensembl

## The Brief

It started with a chicken curry recipe.

The kind that gets shared on Instagram, forwarded on WhatsApp, saved somewhere on Pinterest, and then completely lost the moment you actually want to make it. The recipe existed. It had been seen, shared, and saved. But between three social platforms and two messaging apps, it had effectively disappeared.

Ensembl was the answer to that specific, mundane, universal frustration: a single place to collect content saved from anywhere across social media, organise it into collections, and actually find it again when it matters.

## The Problem

Social media has quietly replaced the browser for a generation of searches. People don't Google date night ideas anymore — they search TikTok. They don't use TripAdvisor — they scroll Instagram. Short-form video turned every platform into a discovery engine, and people adapted.

The problem is that saving content is still platform-native. Save something on Instagram, it lives on Instagram. Save something on Pinterest, it stays on Pinterest. Share a recipe on WhatsApp and it buries itself in a thread. There's no layer that sits above all of it — no place to pull it together, organise it, and make it searchable across the noise.

That's the gap Ensembl was built to fill. Not a social network. Not another platform. Just the organisational layer that should have existed already.

## The Approach

The product concept was clean from the start: save, organise, share. A collection-based structure — date night ideas, recipes, holiday inspiration, whatever — that could be kept private or shared with others. Built for couples, friends, anyone who passes content back and forth and loses it in the process.

The freemium model matched the mission: free to use, with an ad-free one-time payment and a Pro tier for power users who want unlimited collections and higher limits. No subscription required to get value. The pricing was designed to feel honest rather than extractive.

The waitlist approach was deliberate — validate the idea before committing to a full launch. Thirty people signed up organically. The idea had legs.

## The Work

A landing page, waitlist, and pricing structure live at myensembl.com — built on the MM stack: Next.js, TypeScript, Tailwind, Supabase. The mobile application was built in Flutter and came close to App Store submission. The core features were in place: saving content from social platforms, organising into collections, sharing with other users.

The stumbling block was in-app content rendering. The vision was for saved content to play natively inside Ensembl rather than bouncing users back to the original platform. The reality of social media APIs made that harder than anticipated — and the pivot to link-back rather than native playback was a compromise that stalled momentum.

The web version was never built. The team disbanded. The project is shelved — but the website stays live, the waitlist still trickles, and the idea remains sound.

## The Outcome

Ensembl didn't ship. But it validated something worth knowing: thirty organic waitlist signups for a product with no marketing budget and no launch. People understood the problem immediately because they'd lived it.

The design holds up. The concept holds up. The market has moved — other tools now do pieces of what Ensembl planned — but the core insight, that social content needs its own organisational layer, hasn't gone away.

If it comes back, it comes back as a web-first product with a Swift iOS app — built properly, on the right stack, with the lessons of the first attempt already learned.
